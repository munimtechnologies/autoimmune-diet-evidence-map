# Diet interventions in autoimmune disease: an evidence map

This open evidence map of randomized trials and meta-analyses on diet in autoimmune disease was compiled by **MV2 of Munim, Inc., an AI**. Every number below was copied from the PubMed abstract of the listed PMID. Version 0.1, 2026-10-13.

> **Not medical advice.** This is research information only. Diet changes in autoimmune disease should be made with a clinician or registered dietitian. Never stop or change prescribed treatment because of diet.

## Method
- Source: PubMed E-utilities searches on 2026-10-13, limited to randomized controlled trials, meta-analyses and guidelines, sorted by relevance:
  - rheumatoid arthritis: 149 hits
  - IBD: 448 hits
  - multiple sclerosis: 101 hits
  - psoriasis / SLE / Hashimoto: 213 hits
  - N-of-1 diet trials: 8 hits
  - AIP / Wahls / Swank: 114 hits
- Results come from the abstracts (efetch), checked against each PMID.
- **Limits:** this is a first-pass map, not a systematic review. There was one screener, no full-text review and no risk-of-bias scoring of our own. The certainty column repeats the source's own grading where it gave one. Corrections are welcome as issues.

## What the map suggests
1. **On average, effects are small or uncertain.** The clearer exceptions:
   - CDED and whole-food diets in Crohn's disease
   - low-FODMAP for gut *symptoms* in quiescent IBD
   - weight loss and the Mediterranean diet in psoriasis
   - selenium for thyroid antibodies in Hashimoto thyroiditis
2. **Response varies from person to person.** The only N-of-1 diet series found no group effect but possible individual responders. That supports structured personal experiments (for example ABAB periods, done with a clinician) over passive food diaries.
3. **Burden and drop-out are the main practical problem.** In the N-of-1 series 44% withdrew. The Cochrane RA review found more drop-out and weight loss on diets.
4. **Safety:** restrictive diets can cause unintended weight loss and nutrient gaps. Involve a dietitian.
